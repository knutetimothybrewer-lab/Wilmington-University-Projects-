Topic 3

Assistive technology (AT)

📘 IDEA: "any item, piece of equipment, or product system… used to increase, maintain, or improve the functional capabilities of a child with a disability."

Low-tech

  • Pencil grips, slant boards
  • Highlighter tape
  • Graphic organizers
  • Visual schedules

Mid-tech

  • Calculators, timers
  • Audiobooks
  • Word prediction
  • Digital recorders

High-tech

  • Screen readers
  • AAC "talkers"
  • Eye-gaze & switch access
  • Refreshable braille
🔑 Key difference: accessibility features are for everyone; AT is matched to one student, often written into an IEP or 504 plan.
🤖 Where AI fits: smarter word prediction, natural AAC voices, auto image description.
Topic 4

Accommodation vs. modification

Accommodation

Same shelf, new way up. Changes HOW

Modification

The goal itself moves. Changes WHAT

📝 20-word spelling test: Accommodation: take it orally, or with extra time Modification: study 10 words instead of 20

🎓 In college: IEPs end after high school. Disability Services provides accommodations, but course standards (the WHAT) generally stay the same.

Topic 5

Algorithmic bias: how it sneaks in

Algorithmic bias = an automated system that makes errors that consistently favor or harm certain groups.

📚
Past datawho was flagged, placed, or graduated
⚙️
Algorithmlearns the patterns, fair or not
🎯
Score"at-risk," "not gifted," "Level 2"
🧒
Real decisionabout a real student
🛞If past decisions were unfair, the pattern is unfair, and now it's automated and looks "objective."
Shows up in: 📖 Reading & dyslexia screeners⭐ Gifted identification🚨 Dropout early-warning🪜 Course placement🕵️ AI-writing detectors

Stereotypes & missing perspectives

  Chatbot
Write two sentences about a nurse and an engineer at work.
The nurse gently checked on herassumed woman patients before lunch. The engineer reviewed hisassumed man blueprints for the new bridge.
🖼️ Image generator: "a CEO"
Bloomberg (2023): in 5,000+ AI images, high-paying jobs were dominated by lighter-skinned men. Press next to picture the real world.
🕳️ Missing perspectives: ask "Who were the great inventors?" and you may get few women, few disabled inventors, and few people outside the U.S. & Europe.
🔧 Fix it: ask for multiple perspectives, check sources, and always ask: "Whose voice is missing?"
Topic 6 · Dialects

Different ≠ wrong

✍️ My cousin been working at the library. ✏️ Grammar AI: Did you mean "has been"? Not an error!

In African American English, stressed "BEEN" means it's been true for a long time. It's a rule-governed dialect, not a mistake.

0% vs 0%
Speech-recognition word error rates for Black vs. white speakers across 5 major systems.
Koenecke et al., PNAS (2020)
Same idea, lower score
Language models judged identical content more negatively, and suggested less prestigious jobs, when written in AAE.
Hofmann et al., Nature (2024)
Dialect ≠ deficit.  Also true for Southern, Appalachian, Spanglish & World Englishes. Teach code-switching as a choice, not a correction.
Topic 7

ADA & Section 504, digitally

1973
Section 504No disability discrimination in programs that get federal funds, which covers nearly every school & college.
1990
ADATitle II: public schools & colleges. Title III: many private ones.
1998
Section 508Federal agencies' technology must be accessible, and it set the model for schools.
2024
ADA Title II web rulePublic schools' sites, LMS content & apps must meet WCAG 2.1 AA. Deadlines: Apr 2026 (large) / Apr 2027 (smaller).
⚖️ Bottom line: if a school posts it, assigns it, or requires it, including docs, videos, LMS pages, apps & AI tools, students with disabilities must be able to use it. "The AI made it" is not an exemption.
Topic 7 · In practice

WCAG in four blocks

PPerceivableCan they see or hear it? Alt text, captions.
OOperableCan they use it? Keyboard-only, enough time.
UUnderstandableClear language, consistent layout.
RRobustWorks with screen readers & other AT.
✅ Before you post AI-made content:
  • Alt text that conveys meaning
  • Auto-captions checked by a human
  • Real headings & lists, not just bold
  • Contrast ≥ 4.5 : 1
    ✗ hard to read✓ easy to read
  • Descriptive links ("Syllabus PDF," not "click here")
  • Test it with the keyboard only

Put it together

Case study: meet Jordan

Jordan, college freshman
  • ADHD, with a 504 plan (extended time)
  • Speaks African American English at home
  • Uses a free AI app on a phone
  • Takes an AI-scored writing screener for English placement
🧩 BenefitA task-breakdown tool could help Jordan get started.
🔒 LimitNever paste Jordan's 504 details into a public chatbot.
⚖️ Accom.Extended time applies to the AI screener too. It's the same test, with more time.
🎯 BiasThe screener may score AAE features as "errors," which could misplace Jordan in a remedial course.
📜 LawUnder ADA & 504, the screener must be accessible and accommodations honored.
🌐 EquityA phone and a free plan aren't equal access. Offer a lab, a device, or a human-scored option.
