Part 2 · Grounding

What grounding is

Grounding means anchoring the AI's answer to source material you supply, so every claim can be traced back and checked.

NOT GROUNDED GROUNDED

Grounded answers can be checked. Ungrounded answers have to be trusted.

Same question, two answers

Question: "A student turned in an essay 3 school days late with no excused absence. What happens to the grade?"
NOT GROUNDEDIllustrative example

Hover or tap an underlined claim.

No tether to a source

GROUNDEDIllustrative example

Hover or tap a claim or its S-chip to see the source sentence.

"It's not in the source."

Question: "Can the student earn extra credit to make up the points?"
NOT GROUNDEDIllustrative example

Yes, many teachers allow extra credit up to 5% to offset late penalties, usually through an extra assignment. ? invented

The policy says nothing about this.

GROUNDEDIllustrative example

The attached policy does not say whether extra credit can offset late penalties. Not in the source. Please check with the department or the teacher.

Why: the prompt gave an exit: "If the source does not cover something, write 'not in the source.'"

A good grounded prompt gives the model permission to say "not in the source."

    Part 2 · Grounding

    The grounding recipe: build a prompt

    Grounding meter: 0 of 5

    Here is my source: [paste text, standard, or rubric].Create [the version I need] for [grade level and need, with no student names or identifying details].Using only this source, create [the version I need] for [grade level and need, with no student names or identifying details].If the source does not cover something, write "not in the source" instead of adding it.First, quote the lines you will rely on. Then write your answer from those quotes.After each key point, quote the line it came from.Then list anything you were unsure about.

    Grounding vs. what you may hear

    RAG is a technique

    Retrieval-augmented generation fetches documents and adds them to the prompt. Introduced to the research community by Lewis et al., 2020.

    →

    Grounding is the goal

    Every claim traceable to a source you chose.

    Grounded is not the same as factual

    Grounding ties claims to the source you choose. A bad or outdated source gives a confidently wrong grounded answer.

    Part 3 · Putting it together

    Data rule, every time

    ALWAYS
    • Use invented or de-identified data only.
    • Never enter IEP content, disability status, or evaluation results into a general AI tool.
    • No student names, IDs, or records in decks, HTML files, or scripts.
    • Follow your district's AI policy.
    SAFE

    "A Grade 4 student who reads below grade level and needs a glossary."

    NOT SAFE

    A real student's name, ID, or diagnosis pasted into a chat.
